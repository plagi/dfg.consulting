02

Services

Three lines of work. Each starts with a written assessment, continues with an implementation plan, and ends with delivery oversight.

AI strategy and delivery

For organisations that have decided to use AI and have not yet decided how.

We start with a written assessment: which systems, data and contracts are actually there, who owns them, and which use-cases are worth building first. The plan that follows names owners, sequence and budget bands, so the board can approve it and the people who build it can follow it. Then we oversee the build, vendor by vendor, until the first use-case runs in production with a measured result.

  • A ranked shortlist of use-cases, with a business owner for each
  • A plan with owners, sequence and budget bands
  • Vendor selection and contract terms your team can operate afterwards
  • Monthly delivery oversight until the first use-case is live and measured

When to call: a board has given a mandate for AI and there is no plan. A pilot ran and stopped. Several use-cases are running and vendors, cost or the review queue are slipping.

Run the AI readiness check
The assessment8 sections · 20 to 40 pages
  1. 01What is there Systems, data, contracts and people, verified against the source.
  2. 02Who owns what Every system and dataset with a named owner, or the gap where one should be.
  3. 03The use-cases, ranked By expected value and feasibility, on one page.
  4. 04The data What it will cost to make usable, and which use-cases that removes.
  5. 05The vendors What they offer, what they charge, and the contract terms that matter.
  6. 06The risks What could stop the first use-case reaching production.
  7. 07The plan Owners, sequence, budget bands. Written so the board can approve it.
  8. 08The recommendation What to do first, and whether further support is needed.
Always the same eight. Written for the people who approve it and the people who build it.

Fintech product, licensing and bank partnerships

For fintechs deciding what to build, where to launch, on whose licence, and with which partners.

The assessment works out the segment and the product, the business model and its unit economics, the licensing landscape in each target market, and the providers and partners the product will run on. The plan sequences market entry, licensing and partnerships, with owners and budget bands. Then we stay through provider selection, the partnership negotiations and a bank's onboarding review, until the product is live in the market it was planned for. Advisory only; where a regulated step is needed, we bring in the licensed party.

  • Unit economics from measured inputs: pricing, cost to serve, cost to acquire, payback
  • The licensing landscape per market, and what can launch first
  • A provider comparison covering requirements, cost, security assurance and exit terms
  • Partnership design for banks and card networks, and the evidence each will ask for
  • Oversight through negotiation, onboarding and launch

When to call: a founder with a prototype and no pricing. A fintech entering a second market. A product that needs a sponsor bank, a card programme, or a new KYC or payments provider.

Run the fintech readiness check

  1. 01The segment and the product With the customer evidence.
  2. 02The model Pricing, cost to serve, cost to acquire, payback.
  3. 03The market The licensing landscape per market, and where a licensed party is needed.
  4. 04The providers KYC, payments, core, custody: requirements, cost, assurance, exit terms.
  5. 05The partners Banks and card networks, and the evidence each will ask for, in order.
  6. 06The plan Owners, sequence, budget bands, and the metrics to watch.

Security posture and customer due diligence

For companies whose customers, partners or board are asking how secure they are, and who want an answer with evidence behind it.

The assessment assesses your security posture against the frameworks your customers ask about, ISO 27001, SOC 2 and PCI DSS among them, reviews the architecture and the vendors that hold your data, and tests whether the incident-response plan would work on the day. The plan is a gap list in the order a reviewer works through it, with owners and weeks. Then we stay for the remediation, vendor by vendor, and answer the due-diligence questionnaire with you, until the customer signs. We prepare you for audits; we do not perform them.

  • A control-by-control gap list with the evidence a reviewer will ask for
  • Architecture and vendor review with the risks named and the accepted ones written down
  • An incident-response plan rehearsed with the people in it
  • Questionnaire responses with evidence or a remediation date for each
  • Remediation oversight until the evidence exists

When to call: your largest customer has sent a security questionnaire. A bank or partner's third-party review is due. The board wants to know what would happen on the day something goes wrong.

Run the security check
The security assessment8 sections · 20 to 40 pages
  1. 01Scope and assets What holds customer data, where, and who owns it.
  2. 02Threats and risk What would hurt, how likely, and what is consciously accepted.
  3. 03Controls What operates today, set against the frameworks your customers ask about: ISO 27001, SOC 2, PCI DSS.
  4. 04Architecture Identity, network, data and key management, reviewed.
  5. 05Vendors and cloud Who holds your data, and what assurance you hold on them.
  6. 06Incident response The plan, the rehearsal, and who tells the customers.
  7. 07The gap list In the order a reviewer works through it, with owners and weeks.
  8. 08The recommendation What to fix, what to accept, and whether further support is needed.

Method

Three steps, with their durations.

Assessment, implementation planning and delivery oversight. Delivery oversight is the longest step and the one clients retain us for.

Weeks 1-2AssessmentWeeks 3-4PlanningUntil productionOversight

Assessment

Two weeks. Interviews, systems, data and contracts are reviewed against the source. The written assessment records what is in place, what it costs and what is at risk, and opens with the recommendation, including whether further support is needed.

Produces: the assessment report, twenty to forty pages.

Implementation planning

Two weeks. Owners, sequence, budget bands and the vendors to be considered, in one document for the people who approve it and the people who deliver it.

Produces: the implementation plan, and a written quote for delivery oversight.

Delivery oversight

Until production. Oversight of each vendor and workstream until the plan is delivered and the result is measured. A monthly retainer with a notice period. Vendor delays are reported to you as soon as we see them.

Produces: monthly reporting on delivery progress, costs, risks and recommended actions.

Terms

How we work, in writing, before the first call.

Where
Wherever the client is. Remote by default, on site where a room is needed.
Regulation
Advisory only. We don't carry out regulated activity, hold client money or give investment advice. Where a regulated step is needed, we bring in the licensed party.
Price
Fixed scope, fixed price, in USD, agreed in writing before the start. The assessment and the plan are fixed fees; delivery oversight is a monthly retainer with a notice period. A written quote follows the scoping call within three working days.
Vendors
We select and oversee them. No resale, no implementation contracts, no referral fees from anyone we recommend. If a tool you already own meets the need, the assessment says so.
Who
The principal who scoped the work, on every engagement, start to finish. Specialists join for named tasks, a penetration test or a data-engineering sprint, and are named in the scope before you sign.
Confidentiality
A non-disclosure agreement before the first document changes hands, on your paper or ours.
Fit
If another adviser is better suited to the work, we say so in the first conversation and suggest one.
