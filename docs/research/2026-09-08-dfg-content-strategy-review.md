# DFG website: positioning, service language and assessment reasoning

Reviewed 8 September 2026. This is an editorial and commercial review, not a legal opinion or a certification assessment. Recommendations are proposed content, not independently verified claims about DFG's experience or capabilities.

User-confirmed scope: DFG does not provide ISO certification. Certification is excluded from the proposed service offering. The principal focus is the substance and logic of the content, including formulaic AI-style rhetoric, not minor interface defects. A companion [phrase-by-phrase audit](2026-09-08-dfg-language-and-offer-audit.md) identifies exact examples and proposed replacements. “AI-style” describes observable writing patterns; it is not a claim about how the text was authored.

The review covers the live homepage, the six service descriptions, all three self-assessment tools and their result logic, engagement terms, contact wording and supporting legal/privacy text. The live homepage copy matches the local source; deployment adds cache parameters and Cloudflare markup. The live assessment and contact scripts match the local files. Representative assessment journeys were completed without sending an enquiry. Desktop and mobile presentation was inspected. No customer interviews or conversion data were available, so predicted customer reactions are editorial judgments rather than measured findings.

Companion research: [Competitor benchmark](2026-09-08-competitor-benchmark.md).

Snapshot note: local website edits appeared concurrently while these reports were being prepared. This review added research documents only and left those website changes untouched. Findings describe the version inspected in this session, not a completed review of the concurrent rewrite.

## 1. Overall judgment

DFG already has a potentially strong proposition: senior involvement, independent vendor advice, specific deliverables and continuity through execution. Its best material names real purchasing situations: an AI pilot that has stalled, a fintech selecting a sponsor bank, a customer requesting security evidence. Preserve these specifics.

The weakness is the relationship between claims, evidence and conclusions. The website often asserts how organisations work, what boards will decide, what regulators or banks will ask, and how quickly work will finish without enough qualification. The assessments reproduce this voice, turning a few answers into confident explanations of causes they have not established.

Professionalism here should mean precise scope, respectful language and justified recommendations. Merely substituting corporate vocabulary would leave the underlying problems intact.

## 2. Establish one clear commercial position

The current headline communicates attitude before expertise. The supporting text names AI, technology and security; the main services then introduce fintech product strategy, licensing and partnerships. The visitor must assemble the actual proposition across several sections.

The audiences also span boards, CIOs, CTOs, founders, investors, banks, fintechs and corporations. These groups buy different things. A founder validating a product and a bank executive funding AI do not need the same commercial argument.

**Recommended positioning hypothesis:** an independent advisory firm helping banks and fintechs make and execute technology, product and security decisions. This is inferred from the existing service content, not from revenue or customer evidence. If non-financial corporates are a major business line, keep them explicitly in scope and organise the homepage around the three problems instead of an exclusive financial-services identity.

Proposed hero for a financial-services focus:

> **AI, fintech and cybersecurity advisory for financial services.**
>
> DFG helps banks and fintech companies prioritise AI investment, plan products and market entry, and strengthen security. We provide independent advice and oversee delivery with your internal teams and specialist providers.
>
> Senior involvement throughout. Independent vendor advice. Clearly scoped engagements.
>
> Discuss your project · Explore our services

This promises a role DFG can describe and substantiate. It does not imply that DFG builds every system or controls every external approval.

Use the firm's complete name once near its introduction and its legal entity consistently in the footer. Add geographical experience only where named people and engagements support it; a UAE entity does not by itself establish expertise across GCC regulatory regimes.

## 3. Replace subject labels with services a customer can buy

Do not preserve six public services just because six pages exist. “Data and IT foundations” is an internal capability label. “AI strategy” is a subject unless it explains a consequential decision. A service deserves its own page when it has an identifiable buyer, a reason to act, a bounded scope and an outcome the buyer values.

Recommended commercial offers, subject to DFG's actual delivery capability:

| Offer | Reason someone pays for it | What the customer receives and can decide |
|---|---|---|
| AI investment assessment | A sponsor needs to decide whether and where to commit budget | Evaluation of candidate business processes, value assumptions, feasibility, risks and build/buy options; a recommendation to proceed, test, defer or stop |
| AI pilot review and production planning | A pilot consumed time or money but cannot be accepted into normal operations | Evidence-based assessment of the blockage, remaining dependencies, delivery options and acceptance criteria; a decision on the next investment |
| Fintech product and market-entry planning | A founder or product leader is preparing a launch or expansion | Proposition and commercial assumptions, market/operating-model comparison and launch dependencies; a defensible launch or validation plan |
| Banking partner and provider selection | A product needs a partner, or an existing provider is limiting it | Requirements, comparative evaluation, commercial and operational tradeoffs, and onboarding preparation; a supported selection decision |
| Customer security review support | A customer or partner has requested evidence that the company cannot yet assemble confidently | Scope clarification, evidence inventory, supported questionnaire responses and a prioritised gap plan; a clear account of what can be evidenced and what remains open |
| Incident preparedness review, if this is a real delivered service | Leadership needs to know whether critical-service disruption can be managed | Response roles, critical dependencies, exercise findings and improvement actions; decisions about preparedness investment and responsibilities |

Data quality, architecture, integration and system ownership belong inside the AI and fintech offers when they are dependencies. Remove the standalone Data and IT page from the sales menu unless DFG can describe a distinct engagement and buyer demand for it. Merely renaming it “Data and technology readiness” would not solve the commercial problem.

AI strategy can remain a recognised category or search term, but lead the page with why the engagement is worth buying. An AI investment assessment is valuable if it improves a budget decision; producing an AI strategy document is not sufficient value by itself. Avoid presuming that using AI is the correct outcome before evaluating alternatives.

Services, Expertise, Stage and Checks currently reorganise much of the same material. Three domains can remain a browsing aid, with the concrete offers beneath them:

| Main service | Capabilities beneath it | Customer decision |
|---|---|---|
| AI strategy and delivery oversight | AI use-case strategy and governance; data and technology readiness | Where should we invest, and what is needed to deliver and operate it? |
| Fintech product and market-entry advisory | Product strategy and commercial viability; banking partnerships and provider selection | What should we launch, in which market, through which operating model and partners? |
| Cybersecurity and customer assurance | Security readiness and customer reviews; incident preparedness and operational resilience | Which risks and evidence gaps need attention, and how will we demonstrate readiness? |

"Customer due diligence" is ambiguous in a financial-services context: it may suggest assessing customers rather than helping a company respond to a customer's security review. Prefer **customer security reviews** where that is the actual activity. Define **customer assurance** immediately as preparing security evidence for customers and partners.

"Incident response" may attract someone seeking urgent containment or forensic support. The current page mainly describes preparation, plans and rehearsals. Name it **incident preparedness and operational resilience** unless DFG actually provides an emergency response service with a defined response arrangement.

"Licensing" in a service headline can suggest application execution or a legal opinion. Describe the actual work: market-entry planning, understanding dependencies, coordinating qualified advisers and preparing for partner reviews. Identify who delivers legal or regulated elements. Do not imply that DFG can secure an approval or that a partner's permissions automatically cover a proposed product.

### Supporting capability copy, not a requirement to retain six public service pages

**AI strategy, governance and delivery oversight**

> Identify the AI initiatives worth investing in and establish a practical route to production. We assess business value, data readiness, delivery options and governance requirements, then oversee progress with your team and selected providers.

Deliverables: prioritised use-case portfolio, business-case assumptions, readiness findings, governance responsibilities, vendor evaluation and delivery roadmap. Where delivery oversight is included, define reporting, escalation, acceptance criteria and handover. Do not prescribe exactly two use cases before evaluating the business.

**Data and technology readiness — normally an embedded workstream**

> Understand whether your data and systems can support the initiatives you want to deliver. We assess the relevant architecture, data quality, ownership and integration dependencies, and prioritise the improvements needed for the agreed scope.

Deliverables: scoped system/data inventory, architecture and integration findings, ownership gaps, data-quality assessment and remediation priorities. Explain what is sampled or reviewed; avoid an unrestricted promise to verify every system and dataset in two weeks.

**Fintech product strategy and commercial viability**

> Define a fintech proposition with a clear customer need and a credible commercial model. We evaluate the target segment, pricing, unit economics and operating requirements, then develop a market-entry plan with explicit assumptions and decision points.

Deliverables: proposition and segment recommendations, commercial model, sensitivity analysis, market comparison and launch dependencies. Distinguish observed data from assumptions: early-stage businesses may need a testable financial model before measured customer economics exist.

**Banking partnerships and provider selection**

> Select banking and technology partners that fit your product, target markets and operating requirements. We compare capabilities, costs, contractual terms and assurance, and support commercial discussions, onboarding preparation and integration planning.

Deliverables: selection criteria, provider shortlist, comparison matrix, commercial issues for negotiation, evidence register and dependency plan. Preserve the no-referral-fees statement if it remains accurate. Price belongs in the evaluation alongside risk, capability and exit terms; it is not inherently a minor consideration.

**Security readiness and customer reviews**

> Understand your security gaps and prepare clear, supportable responses to customer and partner reviews. We assess the agreed scope, organise available evidence and prioritise remediation with accountable owners.

Deliverables: scoped gap assessment, evidence register, questionnaire support, remediation plan and status reporting. DFG does not provide ISO certification. Do not sell a certification journey or imply an audit-pass outcome. Framework references may inform a scoped customer review where relevant, but they should not define a service DFG does not offer. Completion means the agreed work and evidence are delivered; customer decisions remain external.

**Incident preparedness and operational resilience**

> Prepare your organisation to respond to disruption and recover critical services. We clarify responsibilities, develop response and continuity plans, and facilitate exercises that test decisions, escalation and recovery arrangements.

Deliverables: critical-service dependencies, response roles and escalation, continuity/recovery requirements, exercise findings and improvement plan. State whether technical restore testing is performed by DFG, facilitated by DFG or carried out by the client's team. A tabletop exercise and a successful technical recovery test are different outputs.

## 4. Give each service page a distinct purchasing argument

The current pages repeatedly describe essentially the same work under “What we do”, “What the Diagnosis covers” and “What you get”. Replace this repetition with a decision sequence:

1. **The situation:** the business problem and intended buyer.
2. **The outcome:** the decision or operational improvement the engagement supports.
3. **Our scope:** activities and boundaries, including who implements.
4. **Your deliverables:** specific artefacts and the decisions they enable.
5. **How the engagement works:** client inputs, milestones and dependencies.
6. **Relevant experience:** named expert and relevant case or work sample.
7. **Next step:** a service-specific conversation or self-assessment.

For example, the AI page should answer: “Can you help us choose and justify an investment?”, “Can you assess an existing pilot?”, and “Who handles production delivery?” The fintech page should answer: “Do you assess product viability, market entry, licensing dependencies, partnerships, or all of these?” The cyber page should distinguish reviewing security controls, preparing evidence and running an audit.

Do not put a full report's table of contents on every homepage service block. Show a concise outcome and three useful deliverables; let the service page carry scope detail and a sample output.

## 5. Repair the logic of the engagement model

**The paid assessment and paid plan overlap.** The Diagnosis already includes a plan, owners, sequence and budget bands. The separate Plan phase then sells these again. The buyer cannot tell what the second phase adds.

Recommended distinction:

| Phase | Question answered | Outputs | Completion condition |
|---|---|---|---|
| Assess | What is the situation, and which decision should we make? | Findings, evidence limitations, options and recommended priorities | Agreed assessment scope completed and findings reviewed |
| Plan | How will the chosen option be delivered? | Workstreams, responsibilities, dependencies, budget assumptions and acceptance criteria | Sponsor agrees the execution baseline |
| Oversee delivery | Is execution progressing against the agreed plan? | Delivery reporting, vendor coordination, risk escalation and decision support | Contracted milestones, handover or agreed end of support |

If the actual commercial product is a combined assessment and roadmap, sell that as one initial engagement instead. The website must follow the real offer.

**Do not force every engagement into two weeks plus two weeks.** A focused assessment may fit that duration. A multi-market fintech proposition, broad security estate or live incident does not become equivalent because the template is repeated. State that duration follows scope, access and stakeholder availability. Publish a typical range only when DFG's delivery history supports it.

**Replace page-count value with decision value.** Twenty to forty pages is a format, not a customer outcome. Lead with what the buyer can decide and what their team can execute. A sample decision brief is stronger evidence than a promised page count.

**Define delivery oversight.** The site promises production outcomes while saying it has no implementation contracts. State who builds, who contracts with providers, who accepts delivery and what DFG is accountable for. Proposed wording: “We advise and oversee delivery; implementation is carried out by your internal team and appointed providers. Responsibilities and acceptance criteria are agreed in the engagement scope.” Adjust this if specialists are contracted differently.

**Make success appropriate to the work.** A sound recommendation to stop an uneconomic product, reduce a scope or accept a documented risk can be a successful advisory outcome. “Until it ships”, “until the customer signs” and “until the audit passes” are unsuitable universal completion conditions.

**An unfunded initiative may still need paid decision support.** The assessments sometimes tell readers that they should not hire anyone until they have an owner or budget. Yet identifying a sponsor, building an investment case or validating customer demand can be precisely why an organisation hires an adviser. Recommend an appropriate smaller scope rather than declaring that help has no value. Likewise, strong foundations may justify targeted independent review without requiring a full assessment.

## 6. Replace dismissive certainty with specific, respectful advice

The recurring problem is not informality alone. It is telling the visitor what must be wrong before establishing the facts. Some lines dismiss committees, legal advice, vendor-built systems or early-stage budgets instead of explaining what needs to be checked.

| Current wording or idea | Better wording |
|---|---|
| Consultants who stay for the boring part | Independent advice, with oversight through delivery |
| A mandate without money is an opinion | Confirm funding ownership and the approval route before committing to delivery |
| Committees rank; they do not own | A steering committee can guide priorities; a named sponsor should remain accountable for outcomes |
| The vendor owns you | Clarify internal ownership, operating responsibilities, knowledge transfer and exit arrangements |
| What vendors are not telling you | Commercial assumptions, exclusions, dependencies and contractual risks |
| A lawyer gave a view; a view is not a plan | Translate the legal analysis into launch requirements, dependencies and responsibilities |
| Nobody should be paid to have customer conversations for you | Start by testing customer demand and willingness to pay; specialist research support may be useful where internal capacity is limited |
| A request the board will send back | The business case may need stronger evidence before an investment decision |
| Most incident plans are documents nobody has opened | An effective incident plan needs clear responsibilities and regular rehearsal |

Use consistent terminology throughout: **use case**, **assessment**, **roadmap**, **delivery oversight**, **customer security review**. Define technical terms where nontechnical buyers need them. Professional language should remain plain: replacing “a plan with owners” with “a synergistic transformation framework” would be a regression.

Use “we found” only for reviewed evidence; “you indicated” for an answer; “this may suggest” for interpretation; “we recommend checking” for the next step. Avoid unsupported frequency claims such as “most”, “the commonest”, “rare”, and universal promises about what boards or reviewers accept.

## 7. Redefine what the assessments are for

The free tools and the paid service both use “Diagnosis”, blurring a short self-report questionnaire with professional assessment. Rename the tools:

- AI readiness self-assessment.
- Fintech launch and expansion self-assessment.
- Customer security review readiness check.

Their role should be to help a visitor identify priorities and prepare a useful conversation. They cannot establish root causes, verify controls, predict regulatory decisions or determine a delivery schedule from a handful of answers.

Shared introduction:

> Identify the areas that may need attention before your next investment, launch or customer review. You will receive a summary of your answers, potential gaps and suggested next steps. Results are based on self-reported information and do not verify your readiness. No email is required to view the result.

Keep the current ungated result and voluntary sharing. Clarify the privacy statement: “Your assessment answers are processed in your browser. DFG receives the summary only if you include it in an enquiry and submit it.” The broader phrase “Nothing is stored” is less precise, particularly once a person sends correspondence. This is a communication recommendation, not an opinion on privacy-law compliance.

### AI: distinguish intention, capability and investment readiness

Several current inferences are not warranted:

- Selecting CIO/CTO as the owner does not establish that business owners are absent.
- Selecting a vendor-built pilot does not establish that nobody internally can operate it.
- Saying data blocked a pilot does not establish that nobody owned the data.
- Having more than six use cases does not establish a lack of portfolio visibility.
- A capable engineering team does not, by itself, establish that governance is the remaining constraint.

Ask the missing questions or weaken the conclusions. Recommended dimensions:

| Dimension | Main question | Follow-up if needed |
|---|---|---|
| Business objective | What outcome is the initiative expected to improve? | Is there a baseline and a success measure? |
| Ownership | Who sponsors the work and owns the business outcome? | Who will own the service in production? |
| Funding | What funding stage has the initiative reached? | What evidence is required for approval? |
| Data | Is the necessary data accessible and suitable? | What is known about quality, permissions and limitations? |
| Delivery | Who will build, integrate and operate it? | Are responsibilities and handover agreed? |
| Risk | How will the use case be evaluated and approved? | What review is needed for its actual impact and data? |
| Operations | How will quality, cost and incidents be monitored? | Who can respond when performance changes? |

Branch by actual situation: exploration, investment decision, stalled pilot or operating portfolio. Do not force a generic governance or procurement diagnosis onto every branch. Present next decisions, not a universal sequence ending in a prescribed number of use cases.

### Fintech: establish product and jurisdiction before prescribing a route

The current questionnaire asks about maturity before establishing what the product does, where it operates, who its customers are or which decision is being made. It conflates holding a licence with applying for one, and treats having paying customers as evidence that a viable model exists. A respondent who has legal advice is assigned a planning gap without being asked whether an execution plan exists.

Recommended opening questions:

1. What does the product do: payments, lending, banking services, infrastructure, or another activity?
2. Who are its customers, and in which current or proposed market?
3. Which decision are you preparing for: validation, first launch, partner onboarding, improving an existing business or expansion?
4. What evidence supports demand, pricing and willingness to pay?
5. Which commercial inputs are measured, and which remain assumptions?
6. What is the status of the operating and authorisation route: not assessed, advice obtained, application planned, application in progress, permissions held, or partner arrangement being evaluated?
7. Which partners and providers are required, and which dependencies remain unresolved?
8. What operational, security and financial-crime responsibilities need to be established for the chosen model?

Use this to route the reader to commercial validation, market-entry planning, partnership preparation or operational readiness. Do not hard-code jurisdiction-specific conclusions without maintained expert review. Replace “what can launch unregulated first” with “which activities can be launched under the proposed operating model, subject to appropriate legal review”.

The form should allow a business to be healthy, adequately funded and still need targeted help. Readiness is not the same as a need to purchase the full DFG assessment.

### Security: report evidence readiness, not an assurance verdict

The existing checklist has a useful distinction between documented, operating and evidenced controls. Preserve that idea. Its conclusions exceed the scope of the questions:

- Fourteen positive answers produce a claim that everything a due-diligence review asks about is in place. No evidence has been reviewed and the actual customer questionnaire is unknown.
- Fourteen negative answers produce an approximately three-week remediation estimate. The calculation uses the longest fixed item estimate and assumes all work proceeds concurrently. It has not established resource availability, scope, dependencies or effectiveness.
- Unanswered items become open remediation items, and an incomplete submission can generate a recommendation to hire DFG. Uncertainty is not evidence of an absent control.
- Work is ordered by a fixed list or estimated duration, rather than established business impact, risk and the actual review's requirements.

These are substantive advisory-logic problems, not just form defects.

Ask which service or system is in scope, who is reviewing it, whether their questionnaire is available, and what evidence the respondent can currently provide. Offer **implemented with current evidence**, **implemented but evidence incomplete**, **partly implemented**, **not implemented**, **not sure**, and a justified **not applicable** option.

Show the count of answered items, claimed strengths, evidence gaps and unknowns separately. Use “You report evidence for 10 of the 14 areas covered by this check” rather than a statement that the organisation is secure or will pass. Show priorities for validation; estimate delivery only after scope, dependencies and capacity have been reviewed.

A scoped current-state versus target-state gap analysis is a stronger foundation for a professional assessment than a universal checklist verdict. NIST's Organizational Profiles guidance provides a primary-source reference for that approach; it does not validate DFG's existing tool or its framework mappings. [NIST SP 1301](https://nvlpubs.nist.gov/nistpubs/SpecialPublications/NIST.SP.1301.pdf).

Have a qualified practitioner maintain framework references with versions and applicability notes. A control reference beside a question does not make the checklist a full standards assessment. The legal page already says the checks are simplified; put the same boundary in the result itself, not just the terms.

### A better result structure for all three tools

1. **What you told us:** short, factual summary.
2. **What appears established:** strengths based on the responses.
3. **What needs clarification:** unknowns or inconsistent answers.
4. **Potential priorities:** each tied to an actual response and business consequence.
5. **Suggested next step:** an action the customer can take, with or without DFG.
6. **How DFG could help:** a relevant scoped service, without claiming that a score proves it is necessary.

Example: “You indicated that a pilot has not reached production and that data quality remains unresolved. This may affect the reliability of the proposed use case. Confirm the affected datasets, required quality thresholds and ownership before agreeing the next delivery milestone. A focused pilot review could help establish the remaining dependencies.”

## 8. Add the evidence that makes a small advisory firm credible

The promise of principal involvement is central, but the reviewed content does not identify that principal or show a substantive biography. Broad experience claims need a visible owner.

Add named people, relevant previous roles, areas of expertise, professional profiles and a clear explanation of the specialist delivery model. Distinguish work delivered by DFG from experience gained by its principals in previous roles.

Publish two or three permission-cleared cases, or anonymised cases with sufficient context. Each should state the client situation, DFG's exact role, scope, deliverables and evidenced outcome. If the client relationship is confidential, say what can be shared instead. Never invent client logos, metrics, qualifications or testimonials to fill the design.

Show a redacted assessment brief, decision matrix or roadmap. Label a fictional example as illustrative. This makes the promised output tangible even before a large case-study library exists.

The emphasis on substantive company information and clear labels also accords with NN/g's research on credibility; that research is directional support, not evidence that a particular DFG redesign will increase conversion. [NN/g: Trustworthiness in Web Design](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/trustworthy-design/).

## 9. Reorder the website as a buyer's argument

Recommended homepage sequence:

1. What DFG does, for whom and in what role.
2. Three service pillars, each connected to a customer decision.
3. Relevant experience and the people responsible for the work.
4. How an engagement is scoped and delivered.
5. Common situations, with direct links to the relevant service.
6. Optional self-assessments, with clear limits and useful outputs.
7. A specific invitation to discuss a project.

The current checks appear before evidence of expertise, and Stage, Services and Expertise repeatedly tell the reader where they might fit. One clear service structure and a shorter “When we can help” section are sufficient.

Keep the visual identity if it suits the firm. The dark palette and technical illustration are distinctive; the larger presentation problem is that the website shows more process symbolism and report outlines than people, work and results. Add a real expert, a useful work sample and a relevant case before adding further abstract graphics.

Avoid presenting Idea → Plan → Production → Scale → Resilience as a universal consulting journey. Security, governance and partner constraints can matter before launch; an established company may need a single review rather than a staged transformation programme.

## 10. Make contact and supporting text consistent with the offer

Replace “What is the problem?” with **“What are you planning or trying to improve?”**. Add helpful guidance: “Tell us about your objective, current stage and any important deadline. A short summary is enough.” This accommodates a growth opportunity as well as an urgent issue.

Use **“Discuss your project”** or **“Request an introductory call”** where that is what follows. Describe the first conversation: objectives, fit and whether a scoped engagement is useful. Keep reply and proposal deadlines only if operationally supportable; distinguish the time after an initial enquiry from the time after requirements are understood.

Rename the homepage's “Terms” section **“Working with DFG”** or **“Engagement principles”**. Keep practical buying information there: scoping, fees, principal involvement, specialist roles, independence and confidentiality. Keep contractual website terms in the footer.

Apply a shared terminology and claims review across homepage, service pages, assessments, result text, metadata, contact emails, terms and privacy text. For example, the terms currently name a “bank onboarding review” instead of the fintech readiness tool. More importantly, their statement that the tools are simplified and non-assuring conflicts with confident claims in the results. The substantive promise must be consistent across the journey; a limitation in the footer does not repair an overconfident result.

## 11. Priority order

| Priority | Content decision or change | Reason |
|---|---|---|
| First | Confirm the primary audience, DFG's role and concrete reasons to hire it | Every other page depends on this positioning |
| First | Remove unsupported diagnoses, blanket timelines and approval implications | These weaken the credibility of the advice itself |
| First | Separate self-assessment from paid assessment, and assessment from execution planning | Customers need to understand what they are receiving and buying |
| Next | Remove or merge weak service pages, then write retained offers around decisions, scope, deliverables and responsibilities | Makes each offer understandable and comparable without preserving an arbitrary page count |
| Next | Publish principal credentials and available case evidence | Substantiates the claims of expertise and senior involvement |
| Next | Rebuild assessment questions and results around evidence, unknowns and scoped next steps | Makes the tools useful and proportionate to their inputs |
| Then | Simplify homepage sequence, supporting terminology and contact language | Presents one coherent customer journey |

Before publishing, ask representative buyers to explain in their own words what DFG does, which service they would choose, who implements the work and what they would receive first. Test assessment scenarios with strong, weak, mixed, unknown and non-applicable responses. The acceptance criterion is that conclusions follow from the answers and the customer can explain the offer—not simply that the wording sounds more formal.
