# DFG: exact language patterns and offer-logic audit

8 September 2026. Read alongside the [content strategy review](2026-09-08-dfg-content-strategy-review.md) and [competitor comparison](2026-09-08-competitor-benchmark.md). This examines substantive content, not minor interface bugs. DFG does not offer ISO certification, as confirmed by the user.

Snapshot note: the quotations describe the live/source version inspected earlier in this session. Website files began changing concurrently during report preparation, including some quoted wording. This audit did not make those website edits and does not claim that every quoted phrase remains in the newest worktree. The commercial and reasoning recommendations concern the inspected version; the concurrent rewrite has not been fully reviewed.

“AI-pattern” here means formulaic rhetoric, repeated templates, invented certainty and unsupported inference. These features can be identified in the text; they do not prove AI authorship.

## The systemic problem

The website creates the impression of expertise by repeatedly talking about names, owners, dates, budget lines, documents and vendors. These are useful management concepts, but repeating them does not explain why a customer should hire DFG for a particular problem. The material needs more domain-specific reasoning and less performed authority.

A strong service description connects an expensive or consequential customer situation to a defined decision, explains how DFG improves that decision, and specifies the evidence and outputs. A strong assessment asks for enough information to support its conclusions. Both should be useful even if the customer does not buy.

## Questions that do not logically support the generated conclusion

| Answer selected | Current conclusion or assumption | Why the inference fails | Better question or result |
|---|---|---|---|
| The CIO or CTO owns the AI request | Business owners have not been named | Technical leadership and business sponsorship can coexist | Ask separately who owns the business outcome |
| A vendor built the pilot | Nobody internally can operate it | Build origin does not establish operational capability | Ask who will operate it and whether handover has been completed |
| Data could not be obtained or trusted | Nobody owned the dataset | Access, quality, permissions and ownership are separate issues | Identify which issue prevented progress and what evidence exists |
| Risk/compliance blocked production | There is no approval path; changing the model is not the fix | The use case may have substantive unresolved risks despite a valid process | Ask what concern was raised and whether the decision is documented |
| Four to six use cases run in production | Governance is the constraint | Count does not measure governance or identify a bottleneck | Ask about observed approval delays, responsibilities and monitoring |
| Vendor delivery slips | Nobody owns vendor oversight | A named owner may exist; scope, capacity or dependencies may explain delays | Ask what the documented blockers and escalation arrangements are |
| A fintech is live with paying customers | A viable model exists and expansion is the next question | Revenue does not establish profitability, retention or a desire to expand | Ask the decision being made and the quality of the commercial evidence |
| A legal view exists | A licensing execution plan is absent | One does not establish the absence of the other | Ask what remains unresolved after the legal analysis |
| Own licence held or application in progress | One combined readiness state | These are materially different states | Split the answer and ask only context-appropriate follow-ups |
| A company has not answered a security item | The control is an open remediation gap | Missing information is not proof of missing implementation | Mark as unknown and recommend verification |
| All security controls marked present | The customer review is ready to pass | No documents reviewed; actual review scope unknown | Report self-declared coverage only |
| All security controls marked absent | About three weeks to close the gaps | No estate size, resources or implementation dependencies assessed | Give initial priorities and explain what is needed to estimate effort |

The result text is assembled through predefined rules. That is acceptable if described honestly. “Written from your answers” should not imply a personal expert review. Prefer “An automated summary based on your responses” where the generation method needs explanation.

## Structural logic defects across the website

### An assessment already contains the plan that the next phase sells

Define whether the initial paid deliverable is a recommendation, a detailed execution plan or both. Then remove duplicate promises. A customer should be able to explain exactly what additional value the next phase provides.

### Every problem becomes a vendor-and-ownership problem

The repeated prescription is a named owner, budget line, shortlist, vendor terms and monthly oversight. Important missing alternatives include weak customer demand, an uneconomic proposition, unsuitable technology, unacceptable risk, insufficient operational value and a decision to stop. Advice needs to allow these outcomes.

### The universal project journey is false

Security and resilience do not begin after scale. Market-entry advice may end in a no-go decision. A partnership review may not involve new production software. Replace the universal “until it ships” narrative with completion conditions appropriate to the engagement.

### The website sells breadth without explaining who can deliver it

AI, data, product economics, multi-market licensing, bank negotiations, security frameworks and incident preparedness are a wide scope. Identify the principal's relevant experience, the work DFG directly performs and where another specialist is engaged. A capability without evidence or a defined delivery model should not receive a polished sales page merely to complete a grid.

### The offer and the assessments have different definitions of help

The site sells planning but sometimes tells an unfunded or early-stage organisation that it should not pay for help. It also recommends oversight based on project stage without establishing a delivery problem that oversight can solve. Route to a proportionate offer instead: investment case, focused review, partner evaluation or evidence preparation.

### Certification references imply a service the user says DFG does not offer

Remove audit-pass promises and certification-oriented sales language. The repeated ISO/SOC/PCI references currently make the security offer look partly like audit or certification preparation. Recentre it on the actual service: assessing relevant security risks and helping respond to customer or partner requests. Retain technical framework references only where they are relevant to a specific scope and accurately maintained. Do not add an ISO certification page, package or assessment outcome.

### Repetition obscures missing evidence

“What we do”, “What the Diagnosis covers” and “What you get” often restate the same list. “Stage”, “Services” and “Expertise” repeatedly route to the same areas. Use that space for a sample deliverable, a real engagement, who does the work, what the client contributes and which decisions the work supports.

| AI strategy | Rebuild around investment decisions and stalled-pilot reviews; describe oversight separately |
| Data and IT foundations | Merge into the relevant engagements unless a distinct commercial offer can be demonstrated |
| Fintech product | Lead with validation, launch or expansion decisions; distinguish evidence from assumptions |
| Partnerships/provider ecosystem | Keep, but name partner/provider selection and the specific review support |

| Incident response | Describe preparedness only if that is the actual work; do not imply emergency response |
| Universal Diagnosis/Plan/Stay model | Replace with clearly separated deliverables and engagement-specific completion criteria |
| Three assessment tools | Rewrite questions and conclusions together; polishing questions alone preserves the invalid reasoning |
| Repeated “Do you need us?” verdicts | Replace with relevant next actions and optional, proportionate DFG support |
| Homepage report contents and repeated service sections | Reduce repetition; add people, examples and evidence |
| Contact, terms and privacy copy | Align service names, claims and next-step promises with the rebuilt offer |

## Editorial acceptance test

For each proposed service, a customer should be able to answer: “What situation would make me hire DFG? What decision will improve? What work will they do? What will I receive? Who implements it? Why trust this team?”

For each generated recommendation, a reviewer should be able to point to the answer or verified evidence that supports it. If that support is missing, ask a follow-up, label the uncertainty or remove the recommendation.
