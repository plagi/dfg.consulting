# DFG website: consolidated fix plan

Date: 8 September 2026. Status: implementation specification; no website implementation or deployment is included in this planning task.

## Confirmed delivery-model update

The owner subsequently confirmed that DFG performs implementation both directly and through partners, depending on the task. This supersedes advisory-only restrictions in this original plan. Present assessment, planning, implementation and oversight as available engagement options. Define delivery responsibilities, acceptance criteria and handover per scope; do not imply that every capability is in-house. ISO certification remains excluded.

## Objective

Make the website explain what a customer hires DFG to accomplish, what DFG actually does, what the customer receives and why the team is credible. Rebuild the assessment reasoning around those offers. Professionalism means concrete scope and defensible claims, not more formal synonyms.

This plan supersedes alternative recommendations in the earlier research. It uses the current local source as its baseline: much of the original slogan-heavy wording has already been revised. The broad service names, repeated service structures, universal two-week stages and several unsupported assessment conclusions remain. Preserve useful completed edits.

Sources: [content review](research/2026-09-08-dfg-content-strategy-review.md), [phrase and reasoning audit](research/2026-09-08-dfg-language-and-offer-audit.md), [Advix comparison](research/2026-09-08-advix-direct-competitor.md), [broader benchmarks](research/2026-09-08-competitor-benchmark.md).

## 1. Decisions for this implementation

- Primary customer context: banks and fintech businesses, with other companies included where the described technology or customer-security problem fits. Do not claim additional sector or geographical specialisms without evidence.
- DFG's published role: independent advice and delivery oversight. Identify separately any work performed by internal teams or specialist providers. Do not silently expand the offer into software implementation, emergency response or legal services.
- ISO certification is not offered. Remove certification-led selling and audit-pass promises. Relevant framework references may support a defined customer-review scope, but are not a service in themselves.
- Publish four main service pages containing five distinct initial engagements. Delivery oversight is a separately scoped continuation, not a mandatory third stage.
- Merge Data and IT foundations into relevant service scopes. A future standalone technology service requires a real buyer trigger, defined deliverable and delivery evidence; do not invent one to match Advix's catalogue.
- Put incident preparedness beneath security support rather than presenting it as an emergency incident-response service.
- Keep the visual identity. Change content hierarchy, density and evidence placement; a full visual redesign is not required.
- Keep the three free tools, but rebuild their questions and result rules. Results remain available without providing an email.

Advix informs the connection between customer situation, concrete work and case evidence. It does not determine DFG's service breadth. Its [IT offers](https://www.advix.com/services/it-strategy) and [Octobank case](https://www.advix.com/cases/octobank) are useful references for making work tangible; its inconsistent [cybersecurity package descriptions](https://www.advix.com/services/cybersecurity) are a counterexample.

## 2. Target offers and page structure

| Page / offer | Buyer and trigger | DFG's work | Initial deliverable and decision |
|---|---|---|---|
| AI investment assessment | Sponsor evaluating proposed AI spending | Review candidate processes, expected value, data/integration requirements, risk and build/buy alternatives | Investment recommendation with assumptions and options: pursue, test, defer or stop |
| AI pilot review — on the same AI page | Product or technology leader whose pilot has stalled | Review pilot evidence, success criteria, deployment barriers, costs and operating responsibilities | Findings and production options; recommendation on further investment and remaining dependencies |
| Fintech product and market-entry planning | Founder or product leader preparing a launch or expansion | Review demand evidence, proposition, unit economics, market options, operating model and partner dependencies | Commercial model and market-entry decision brief, followed by an agreed launch/validation plan if commissioned |
| Banking partner and provider selection | Team selecting or replacing a bank, processor or other provider | Define requirements, compare capabilities and costs, evaluate operational/contractual dependencies, prepare for onboarding | Selection matrix, recommendation and onboarding requirements; client retains the appointment decision |
| Customer security review support | Company responding to a customer or partner's security request | Clarify scope, review available evidence, identify gaps and support accurate questionnaire responses | Evidence register, supported responses and prioritised actions; no assurance or approval guarantee |

Incident preparedness is an optional security workstream: response roles, escalation, critical dependencies and exercises. Publish its extent only where delivery capability is supportable. Data quality, architecture and integration reviews are scoped workstreams within AI and fintech engagements.

For each offer, explicitly state client inputs, DFG responsibilities, deliverable format, exclusions and completion conditions. Do not put assumed prices, fixed durations or invented outcomes into the copy.

### AI page: draft opening

> **Decide where AI is worth investing in—and what it will take to deliver.**
>
> DFG helps you evaluate proposed AI initiatives and review pilots that have stalled. We assess business value, data and integration requirements, delivery options and operational risks, then recommend the next investment decision.

Show two offer blocks: investment assessment and pilot review. Below them, explain separately scoped oversight. Remove the assumption that using AI is already the right decision, arbitrary promises to reduce every shortlist to two use cases and universal production deadlines.

### Fintech page: draft opening

> **Plan a fintech launch around customer demand, commercial viability and operating requirements.**
>
> DFG helps you assess the proposition, compare target markets and identify the partners and dependencies needed to launch. The work distinguishes tested evidence from assumptions and makes the next decisions explicit.

Separate first launch from expansion. Explain the role of qualified legal/regulatory advisers when needed; do not imply that DFG issues legal opinions or secures permissions. Do not treat paying customers as proof of a sustainable model.

### Partnerships page: draft opening

> **Choose banking and technology partners that fit your product and operating model.**
>
> DFG defines selection requirements, compares providers and supports commercial evaluation and onboarding preparation. Recommendations consider capabilities, total cost, operational responsibilities and exit arrangements.

Explain selection versus negotiation support versus onboarding preparation. State who contracts with the provider and who integrates the service. Preserve the no-referral-fees claim only if accurate.

### Security page: draft opening

> **Prepare clear, supportable answers to customer security reviews.**
>
> DFG helps you understand the request, organise available evidence and address the gaps that matter to the review. You receive documented responses and a prioritised action plan for the agreed scope.

Explain customer evidence support, scoped security review and optional preparedness work separately. Avoid implying technical testing, certification or emergency response merely through broad cybersecurity terminology.

### Required service-page contents

Use this editorial checklist without forcing identical prose on every page:

1. Customer situation and consequential decision.
2. Defined engagement options and who each is for.
3. Work performed and tangible outputs.
4. Inputs, responsibilities and completion criteria.
5. Relevant expert and case/work sample where available.
6. Contextual enquiry and optional relevant self-assessment.

Remove duplicate lists currently spread across “What we do”, “What the assessment covers” and “What you get”. Activities and outputs should provide different information.

## 5. Assessment redesign specification

### Shared rules

- A question captures one fact; it must not combine materially different states.
- Company stage, job title, vendor involvement and use-case count are context, not scored deficiencies.
- Record known strengths, confirmed gaps and unknowns separately. Allow not applicable when meaningful, with a reason.
- A recommendation must identify the supporting answer. Ask a follow-up when the inference requires another fact.
- Remove fixed remediation-week calculations, deadline-fit predictions and numerical thresholds that imply the respondent should hire DFG.
- Do not replace categorical overclaims with “typically” or “indicatively” while preserving the unsupported conclusion.
- Use transparent conditional rules, not a new generative-AI dependency. No maturity score or peer benchmark unless there is a documented, supportable methodology.
- Keep answers local until a user explicitly submits an enquiry. Display and allow editing of the included summary.
- AI and fintech result pages must offer answer review, not just a full restart. This is necessary to inspect and correct the basis for advice.

Shared result structure:

**Your objective → what you reported → strengths → gaps and unknowns → suggested next actions → optional relevant DFG support.**

Shared boundary wording:

> This automated summary is based on your answers. It identifies topics to investigate; it does not verify readiness or predict approval, implementation cost or delivery time.

### AI tool

Title: **AI project self-assessment**. First ask the decision: evaluating an investment, reviewing a stalled pilot or managing operating use cases. General exploration can route to the investment branch without assuming committed funding.

| Branch | Facts to establish |
|---|---|
| Investment | Business process/objective; baseline and success criteria; evidence of value; sponsor and funding decision; required data/access; delivery and operating options; relevant risk review |
| Stalled pilot | Expected versus observed result; documented blockage; data/integration constraints; production costs; unresolved risk findings; operational ownership; options already considered |
| Operating portfolio | Actual objective or problem; cost and quality evidence; ownership; incident monitoring; vendor dependencies; existing reporting and approval arrangements |

Follow up separately on business ownership, operating capability or approval arrangements. A technical sponsor does not prove business ownership is missing. A blocked risk review does not prove the approval process is absent. Allow no material gap, targeted verification, pause and stop recommendations.

### Fintech tool

Title: **Fintech launch and expansion self-assessment**. Establish product activity, target customer, current/target market and intended decision before offering conclusions.

Branch into validation, launch, expansion or partner selection. Ask about demand evidence, measured versus assumed economics, operating requirements, relevant advice already obtained, and outstanding partner dependencies. Split permissions held, application in progress and partner arrangements. Skip expansion questions for a company seeking to improve its current operation.

Do not generate jurisdiction-specific authorisation advice. Identify the question to resolve with an appropriate adviser. Output commercial unknowns, operating dependencies and the next decision—not a verdict that the business is viable.

### Security tool

Title: **Customer security review readiness check**. Establish the reviewer, service/system scope, whether the actual request is available and the deadline as context only.

For each relevant area, distinguish: implemented with current evidence; implemented with incomplete evidence; partly implemented; absent; not sure; not applicable with reason. Unanswered remains incomplete, not absent.

Report coverage among applicable answered items and display unknown/unanswered counts separately. “All covered” means only all covered by this self-check. Remove automatic week estimates and certification or approval implications. Next actions should concern scope confirmation, evidence collection and substantiated gaps. Do not automatically prioritise by checklist position or estimated duration.

## 9. Acceptance criteria

### Commercial/editorial review

- A reader can identify the relevant offer, why they would buy it, what they receive and who does the implementation.
- Every service promises a decision or defined work outcome, not just a report or broad improvement.
- No universal two-week schedule, fixed shortlist size, automatic need-for-consultant verdict or approval guarantee remains.
- ISO certification is not presented as a service. Security scope is consistent in the page, tool, result and enquiry.
- No unsupported prevalence claims, invented numerical precision, dismissive slogans or outcome claims without evidence.
- Shared wording is consistent; each page still contains distinct domain-specific reasoning.
- Claims already cleaned up in the current source are not reintroduced from old audit quotations.

### Meaningful assessment scenarios

| Scenario | Required result |
|---|---|
| CTO leads an AI initiative; business sponsor is also named | No missing-business-owner finding |
| Vendor built a pilot; an internal team operates it | No unsupported operating-capability gap |
| Risk review rejects a use case through a documented process | Unresolved risk is reported; no invented missing approval path |
| Several AI use cases operate with effective governance | Project count alone produces no governance deficiency |
| Budget not approved because the business case is being assessed | Investment assessment is a relevant option, not an automatic refusal to help |
| Fintech has revenue but economics are untested | Revenue is recorded without declaring commercial viability |
| Fintech holds relevant permissions versus has an application pending | Different contextual dependencies; no legal approval prediction |
| Respondent is not entering another market | No forced expansion recommendation |
| Security answers are incomplete or unknown | Unknowns are separated from confirmed gaps |
| Every security item is marked present | Self-reported coverage only; no assurance or pass verdict |
| Every security item is absent | Priorities to investigate; no invented remediation schedule |
| Respondent changes earlier answers | Irrelevant branch answers and conclusions disappear; reviewed/shared summary reflects current answers |

Use representative rule-level tests for these consequential inferences, plus browser journeys for editing and sharing summaries. Do not write tests solely to lock in decorative text or layout. Verify enquiry behaviour with intercepted/mock delivery rather than sending unsolicited messages.

### Release verification

Check retained routes, legacy redirects, internal links, sitemap, canonical metadata and shared labels. Confirm mobile reading order and that assessment results are understandable without reading the terms. Verify that completing a tool does not submit answers. No placeholder expertise, fabricated cases or half-finished service blocks may be published.

## First implementation milestone

Complete the five offer definitions and four service-page drafts before polishing the homepage. Those definitions are the source of truth for the assessments, navigation, enquiry text and proof requirements. A more attractive homepage cannot repair an undefined offer.
