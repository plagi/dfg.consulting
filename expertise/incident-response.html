Incident response and resilience

Most incident plans are a document nobody has opened. We write one with roles, escalation and a contact list, test it with the people who would use it, and put the critical third parties on file with their own assurance.

Service line: Cybersecurity consultingDiagnosis 2 weeksPlan 2 weeksStay until it ships

The day something goes wrong

We work out what happens today on the day something goes wrong: who is called, what is backed up, what can be restored, and how long it takes.

We write the incident-response and business-continuity plans with named roles and an escalation path.

We list the critical third parties, cloud, KYC vendor, processors, and put each one's assurance on file.

We rehearse the plan with the people in it, and fix what the rehearsal finds.

What the Diagnosis asks

  1. 01Who is called, in what order, and whether they know it
  2. 02What is backed up, whether a restore has ever been tested, and where the copies are
  3. 03Which third parties can take you down, and what assurance you hold on each
  4. 04What the board would be told, by whom, and when
  5. 05The controls an auditor will sample for A.5.24 and A.8.13

Until it has been rehearsed

Until the plan has been tested within the last twelve months and the findings closed. Then a rehearsal a year, if you want us back for it.

Who calls us

A board that wants to know what would actually happen. A CTO after a near-miss. A fintech whose bank has asked for a tested plan.
