Services / AI decisions

AI investment and pilot reviews

Decide where AI is worth investing in and what it will take to deliver. DFG evaluates proposed initiatives and reviews pilots that have stalled, so your next investment has a clear basis.

Before you commit the budget

A promising demonstration is a starting point. The investment decision also needs a business objective, credible value assumptions and a realistic view of delivery and operating costs.

AI investment assessment

We review the processes you want to improve, compare candidate use cases and examine data, integration and risk requirements. We consider existing tools and non-AI alternatives alongside build and buy options.

You receive

An investment decision brief

  • The value case, with measured inputs separated from assumptions.
  • Feasibility and delivery options, including dependencies and cost assumptions.
  • A recommendation to pursue, test, defer or stop, with the evidence needed for the next decision.

When a pilot has not reached production

The next question is whether more investment will resolve the problem. We start with the pilot’s actual results and the reasons deployment stopped.

AI pilot review

We examine success criteria, data and integration constraints, production costs, unresolved risk findings and operating responsibilities. The review distinguishes documented blockers from explanations that still need testing.

You receive

A recommendation on the next investment

  • A findings register linked to pilot evidence and unresolved questions.
  • Options for continuing, changing scope or stopping the initiative.
  • If continuation is supported: remaining dependencies, ownership and acceptance criteria for production planning.

Review the foundations the use case depends on

Data and architecture work is scoped to the initiative. We review whether the required datasets are accessible and suitable, how systems will connect, and who owns each dependency. The output identifies what needs to change before a delivery commitment can be made.

Where implementation is commissioned, DFG can deliver the agreed data and system work directly or with delivery partners. The scope defines responsibilities, testing and handover requirements.

What we need to make the review useful

Bring the business objective, any pilot results or supplier proposals, the current cost assumptions and access to the people responsible for the process, data and technology. We agree which evidence can be reviewed and record access or sampling limitations.

The initial engagement is complete when the agreed scope has been reviewed and the findings and recommendation have been discussed with your sponsor. It does not depend on approving an AI purchase.

If the decision is to proceed

Execution planning translates the chosen option into workstreams, responsibilities, budget assumptions and acceptance criteria. DFG can then implement the agreed solution directly or with partners, including the scoped testing and handover. Delivery oversight is also available when another team implements.

You can commission implementation against an existing plan; a DFG assessment is not a mandatory first stage. The proposal identifies who delivers each workstream, acceptance criteria and any support after handover.
